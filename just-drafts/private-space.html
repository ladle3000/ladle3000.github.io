Private space and encryption

Updated October 19, 2026

Keep personal notes out of ordinary browsing

Private space is a separate area inside Just Drafts. Its notes do not appear among ordinary notes or in ordinary search. Open it using the shield at the bottom of the drawer, then use your separate privacy PIN/password or enabled biometrics. You get the same note editor and private search. Back or the exit arrow closes and locks the session.

For a more discreet entrance, turn on Hide Private space shortcut under Settings → Privacy. The shield disappears from the ordinary drawer. Settings → Privacy still opens the same authenticated private area. Closing it returns to ordinary notes with the shortcut still hidden. This makes access discreet; it is not a guarantee that the feature's existence cannot be discovered.

A lock and encryption do different jobs

A lock controls access through an app's interface. An edit lock prevents changes. Encryption also scrambles stored contents so the underlying files cannot be read as ordinary note text without the right key. Just Drafts combines a private access gate with encrypted private storage. A lock icon in another app does not by itself tell you whether its stored files or backups are encrypted.

We offer this because personal notes may contain things you do not want visible during everyday browsing, and backups should protect that content too. Protection applies to the note and its contents as a whole. There are no separate private passwords for individual checklist items or blocks.

Backups need encryption too

Version 0.98 does not include Private space notes in cloud backups. Encrypted private backup support is being added after that version. Check the version and backup status in the app before relying on a cloud copy.

In builds with private backup support, enable encrypted backups, connect Google Drive or Dropbox, and confirm that a backup completes successfully. Private notes, attachments and saved history are then included in that encrypted backup. While private notes remain, backup encryption cannot be turned off. Move every private note out of Private space or delete it before choosing unencrypted future backups. Older backup copies follow the provider and app's retention behavior.

If no backup service is connected, or a backup has not completed successfully, your new private changes may exist only on this device. Encryption alone does not create a backup. Do not uninstall the app or erase its data expecting encryption to preserve a device-only copy.

Keep the two passwords straight

  • Privacy PIN/password: opens Private space and authorizes its security/recovery settings. Optional compatible biometrics provide another way to enter.
  • Backup recovery password: encrypts cloud backups and is needed to restore them. It is separate from your privacy PIN/password.

After restoring an encrypted private backup, unlock Private space with the private credential saved with that backup, or use its separately saved recovery key through Privacy settings. Biometrics belong to the device and need to be enabled again. An older backup may contain an older private credential or recovery-key configuration.

What the recovery PDF does

The recovery PDF contains a recovery key, never your actual privacy PIN/password. It can help restore access to private storage, but it does not contain copies of your notes and does not replace a backup. Keep it somewhere you trust. Replacing or disabling a recovery key makes older PDF copies unsuitable for the current private credential state; older backups may retain their earlier recovery configuration.

Keep your backup password and recovery information safe. The developer cannot bypass encryption or recover a forgotten backup password.
